Garden design · Los Angeles

Gardens thathold you.

Comfort Zones designs gardens for Southern California as it is now: shaded, alive with birds and bees, beautiful through the dry months, and built to keep the rain when it comes.

The approach

A garden should feel like the most comfortable room of the house, and ask very little water to stay that way.

Not a desert, and not a lawn. A place with shade to sit in and shelter from the wind, cooler than the street, full of life, that looks after itself through a long summer and a short winter.

01

Shade and shelter

Trees and walls placed so the garden is the coolest room you own by July.

02

Rain that stays

Roofs, slopes and paths that send the winter rain into the ground and into cisterns, not the gutter.

03

Living soil, living garden

Healthy soil, native and climate-wise plants, and food and cover for birds, bees and butterflies.

04

Beautiful every month

Grasses that catch the low sun, aloes that flower in winter, colour that does not depend on the hose.

How a garden comes together

Five steps, at the garden's pace

01 / 05
01

Listen

A walk around the place together: how you want to live outside, where the light falls, what you love already.

02

Read the land

Sun and shade, soil, slope, and where the water goes when it rains.

03

Design

A plan that ties the hardscape, grading, plants, rain capture and irrigation into one garden.

04

Build

With trusted builders, on site through the details that make it feel finished.

05

Grow

Seasonal care and coaching, so the soil and the garden get better every year.

Marilee Kuhlmann

A landscape consultant and sustainable garden designer who believes a beautiful garden and a thrifty one are the same garden.

She teaches edible gardening, water conservation and sustainable design, is a past district president of the Association of Professional Landscape Designers, and founded the Green Gardens Tour to benefit Santa Monica's kids. She has served on the Los Angeles Community Forest Advisory Committee and the board of the Southern California Horticultural Society, and writes on water-wise design for Pacific Horticulture and California Landscape Design. Comfort Zones is her studio.
